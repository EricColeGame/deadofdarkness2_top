The 2 GB install size also suggests the game is not built around enormous 4K texture packages.

The real obstacles are business and engineering ones, not silicon ones. Porting still requires certification, controller remapping, save-system work, and QA time — all of which cost money a small studio has to justify.

## Why No PS4 Version Has Been Announced (Yet)

There are several practical reasons a PlayStation 4 release may not be on the table, and none of them are permanent.

- **Self-publishing limits bandwidth.** Retrofiction Games develops and publishes the title itself. A two-platform simultaneous launch doubles the QA and certification workload for a team that is already building a large, lore-heavy game.
- **PC-first is the standard indie pattern.** Console versions of independent horror games frequently arrive months or years after the PC release — and sometimes never arrive at all.
- **PS4 is a legacy platform.** New releases for the base PS4 have slowed dramatically as studios shift to current-gen hardware. A small team may reasonably decide the audience is not there.
- **No publishing partner announced.** A console port is often funded by a porting specialist or publisher deal. No such partnership has been announced for this title.

None of this rules out a PlayStation 4 version later. It simply means nothing is promised, and planning around a PS4 purchase today would be a gamble.
